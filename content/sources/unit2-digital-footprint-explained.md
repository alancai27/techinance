**Digital Footprint Explained**  
In the last lesson, we reviewed how a **digital footprint** can impact your future and how you can influence people through your Internet activities. The main purpose of this lesson is to help you understand how a **digital footprint** affects your own privacy.  
---

**Digital footprint** not only includes your activities on the Internet but also your **personal information** which can be used in harmful ways. All **digital footprints** are divided into active and passive. Let’s look at some examples for each one of them.  
---

Social media, online shopping, online banking, and downloads are all examples of an **active digital footprint**. **An active digital footprint** is all the information that you leave on the Internet with the understanding that this **data** will stay there forever. It can be altered and some part of your **digital shadow** can be deleted. However, it is nearly impossible to fully delete your **digital footprint** because of how many activities you have done. Every single activity is stored somewhere.   
---

**Search history**, **IP addresses**, **device information**, **browser and website cookies** are components of a passive digital footprint. **Passive digital footprints** include the information you leave on the Internet without realizing it. It is common to see website notifications referencing “cookies”. Those “cookies” are actually your **personal information**. Different websites and browsers might collect different data but all of them have the same name. While cookies themselves are not inherently harmful, they can introduce privacy issues as they track and collect data. That is why you should be mindful of that and disable collecting of your information when you can. **In fact, only 26% of websites secure their cookies, leaving the rest at a high risk of cyberattacks.**  
---

Another very important detail that you should keep in mind is that there are a lot of **hackers** who get paid for stealing your information. Please be mindful of this fact and do not leave your address, phone number, or any other **personal information** on suspicious websites. 

**Key Terms and Facts:**

* **Digital footprint** **can also be called digital shadow or electronic footprint.**  
* **Only 26% of websites secure their session cookies, leaving the rest at a high risk of cyberattacks.** That means that most of our information that is collected by using cookies is at high risk.  
* **Digital footprint:** A unique set of your activities on the Internet, which includes your emails, search history, posts, personal information, text messages, etc.  
* **Personal information:** Any information that is directly related to you.  
* **Data:** Facts and statistics collected together for further reference or analysis.  
* **Active digital footprint:** Information that users deliberately share about themselves by using social media or different websites.   
* **Passive digital footprint:** Information that users share about themselves without their knowledge.  
* **Search history:** Lists of websites that a user has visited, as well as time of the visit.  
* **IP address:** A unique number assigned to each computer that uses the Internet.  
* **Device information:** Technical data about the device that you are using.  
* **Browser or website cookies:** Text files with small pieces of data (like a username and password) that are used to identify your computer as you use the Internet.   
* **Hacker**: A person who gains access to programs or information without authorization.
