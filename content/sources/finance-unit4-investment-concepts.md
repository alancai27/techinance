**Basic Investment Concepts**

In today's world, understanding basic investment concepts is necessary for anyone looking to build wealth and achieve financial freedom. Investments can take many different forms, including **stocks, bonds, ETFs, gold,** and more. Each investment type has different levels of risk and **growth potential**, so a well-rounded knowledge of these investment options can help individuals make financially smart decisions that align with their goals.

**Key Investment options** 

1. **Stocks:** A stock is simply just a share in the ownership of a company. When you buy a stock in Apple you technically become a partial owner of the company along with everyone else who owns Apple stock. This means you are entitled to a portion of that company's profits or can suffer from the company's losses. The risk level of individual stocks can vary. Still, they tend to be high as stocks can fluctuate in value significantly based on market conditions, economic environment, companies' financials, inner workings, etc. However, the growth potential of stocks can be very high, especially over a long-term investment horizon which mitigates risk. You make returns from stocks through **capital gains** (increase in the stock price over time) and **dividends** (a portion of the company profit paid to shareholders a specific amount of times per defined cycle)  
     
2. **Bonds:** A bond is a debt security issued by corporations, municipalities, or governments. More simply, by buying a bond you’re giving the issuer a loan and they agree to pay you back(an IOU) the face value of the loan on a specific date plus periodic **interest payments** along the way, usually twice a year. Bonds are a much safer investment than stocks and you can almost guarantee a return on them of usually 2-4%. However, the return potential from bonds is much less than from stocks. Bonds are most suitable for investors looking for steady risky returns rather than risky growth returns.  
     
3. **ETFs:** An ETF is simply a basket of different investments, typically stocks. For instance, the SPDR S\&P 500 Trust ETF is an ETF that encompasses all companies in the S\&P 500 which is a stock index comprised of the 500 largest public companies in the U.S. By investing in an ETF you are essentially diversifying your portfolio at a fraction of the cost giving you strong stable returns with less risk than investing in individual stocks. Another investment type like an ETF is a **mutual fund** however they are not as good for several reasons. First of all, they are actively managed and 80% of managers can’t beat the market; in addition to this, they have higher fees and less flexibility in your investment making ETFs the superior investment. An ETF risk level can vary depending on the type of investments in the ETF but they tend to have a risk level higher than bonds but lower than individual stocks. ETFs provide moderate to high returns and are terrific investments for investors looking to have lower risk while still having the potential for higher returns.  
4. **Mutual Funds**: A fund that pools money from many investors and invests the money into stocks, bonds, and short-term debt.   
5. **Gold:** Gold is considered one of the safest investments you can make with incredibly low risk and a pretty strong return given the low risk. Since 2000 gold has returned an average of 6.3% annually adjusted for inflation and given its incredibly low risk which is comparable to bonds it makes gold a terrific long-term investment. Gold also can act as a **hedge against inflation** or currency devaluation as it is an asset sought after in times of economic uncertainty.

**Understanding risk tolerance vs. growth potential**  
All investors need to understand their level of **risk tolerance** to make smart investment decisions for themselves. Risk tolerance is an investor's comfort level with potential losses in exchange for higher returns as it tends to be that the more risk involved with an asset the greater the potential for high returns or high losses. A key rule of thumb that many investors use is that if you are losing sleep or constantly worrying about an investment its risk is too high for your personal risk tolerance. This is why even though stocks offer the potential for high returns many investors tend to stick towards safer investments like bonds and gold. However many investors have no issue investing in individual stocks with high risk albeit through research and financially smart investing otherwise it's just gambling. However, balancing your overall portfolio to include assets from several different investment classes like the ones listed above will most likely provide you with a risk tolerance level you are comfortable with while still making good overall returns.

**Importance of long-term investing**  
Investing for the long term is a tried and tested method that is guaranteed to grow your investment exponentially. This is due to **compounding** returns where earnings generate additional income on top of the income you have already earned. Also over the long term it is almost guaranteed that the value of your investment will rise while in the short term, it is very hard to predict what will happen with any investment even low-risk ones. Investing the long term is generally considered anything over 5 years, however the longer you hold an investment the less risk there is and higher growth potential as well. No matter the type of investment a long-term investment horizon is always the most viable and financially smart strategy.

**Key Terms and Facts**

* **Stock:** A security representing partial ownership in a company, with potential for capital gains and dividends.  
* **Bond:** A debt security where the investor lends money to an issuer (corporation, municipality, or government) in exchange for periodic interest payments and repayment of the loan’s face value at maturity.  
* **ETF (Exchange-Traded Fund):** A type of fund composed of multiple assets, typically stocks, that offers diversified exposure with lower fees and more flexibility compared to mutual funds.  
* **Mutual Fund:** A pooled investment that is actively managed, generally has higher fees and may underperform the market, unlike many ETFs.  
* **Gold:** A precious metal valued for its intrinsic worth and used as a hedge against inflation and currency fluctuations.  
* **Risk Tolerance:** The level of risk an investor is willing to accept in exchange for potential financial gains. Higher risk tolerance allows for a more growth-oriented portfolio.  
* **Growth Potential:** The anticipated rate of return for an investment. Investments with high growth potential, like stocks, carry more risk.  
* **Capital Gains:** The profit earned from the sale of an asset, such as a stock, when its selling price exceeds its purchase price.  
* **Dividends:** Portions of a company’s profits distributed to shareholders, typically regularly.  
* **Interest Payments:** Payments made to bondholders as compensation for lending funds to the bond issuer.  
* **Compounding:** The process where investment earnings generate additional income over time, leading to exponential growth.  
* **Hedge Against Inflation:** An investment strategy, often involving assets like gold, used to protect against the loss of purchasing power due to inflation.
