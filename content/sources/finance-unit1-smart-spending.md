Being able to effectively manage your finances is a fundamental skill that is
essential to all aspects of daily life, whether it be deciding to eat out or meal
plan, planning a vacation, or even deciding if you want to buy the latest gaming
console. Learning how to make informed financial choices early on is very
important when you have to make a big decision later on in life such as taking
out a college loan or a mortgage, and can mean the difference between financial
freedom or a life of living paycheck to paycheck. It may seem difficult to
imagine how you can make these decisions, however, it ultimately starts with the
"smart spending habits" that you have developed over time. Well what does "smart
spending" look like?

Smart spending refers to planning and prioritising where your money is spent and
directing it toward meaningful and necessary goals, or in other words, maximizing
the value from your money, and it consists of various strategies and habits that
will help you determine if a purchase is worth your money.

## Key Strategies

1. **Combat Impulse Buying**
   - Ever notice that when you go to the grocery store with a list of items, you
     only end up buying the items on the list, without carelessly adding other
     items to your cart.
   - This is the primary way to combat impulse buying, or recklessly purchasing
     items that seem intriguing or that you like.
   - When making a big purchase, try the 48-hour rule; wait two days before you
     reach a final decision so that you can decide if the item is worth it
2. **Track your spending:**
   - Being able to track both your income and expenses is crucial because it
     allows you to determine where you should prioritize your money, reducing
     "pleasure purchases"
3. **Categorise your expenses:**
   - Categorizing your expenses into groups like rent, utilities, groceries, and
     entertainment makes it easier to identify which groups are taking up too
     much of your budget, allowing you to readjust spending
   - Money management apps like Quicken and Rocket Money do this process for you
     automatically by sorting and tracking expenses as well as providing an
     overview of spending patterns
4. **Prioritise needs vs. wants**
   - When developing good spending habits, it important to be able to
     differentiate what you need (essential expenses) vs. what you want (impulse
     buying)
   - Making a list of your essential costs allows you to step back and think
     about how much of the money that you "need" to spend before you can allocate
     any to what you "want" to get
5. **Explore different budgeting strategies:**
   - 50/30/20 rule (typical budgeting strategy):
     - 50% of income goes to necessities (housing, utilities, groceries)
     - 30% of income goes to wants (entertainment, eating out)
     - 20% of income goes to savings or debt repayment
   - 60/30/10 rule (for those with higher costs, such as housing or childcare):
     - 60% of income goes to necessities
     - 30% of income goes to wants
     - 10% of income goes to savings or debt repayment

## Good Habits

1. Stick to a budget
2. Track expenses
3. Create an emergency fund
4. Create a shopping list when you go out
5. Compare prices across stores
6. Utilize coupons and promotions
7. Meal prep/Pack your own food
8. Think before you buy
9. Look for free activities

## Habits to Avoid

1. Overusing credit cards
2. Impulse buying
3. Shopping without a list
4. Shopping for status
5. Spending more than you earn
6. Too many non-essential subscriptions
7. Not tracking small expenses

## Key Terms and Facts

- **Budgeting:** Methods for managing income, differentiating between needs and
  wants, and creating a budget to ensure financial stability.
- **Finances:** All the money you earn, spend, save, and invest (e.g. income,
  expenses, savings, debts, and investments)
- **Impulse buying:** Making a purchase without planning or thinking it through
- **Comparison Shopping:** Looking at different stores or websites to find the
  best price and quality before buying something
- **Emergency Fund:** Money set aside for unexpected expenses like medical bills,
  car repairs, or job loss
- **Essential Expenses:** Costs for basic needs you must pay to live and work
  (e.g. housing, groceries, transportation, and utilities)
- **Cash Flow:** The movement of money in and out of your finances (Positive cash
  flow means you're earning more than you spend: negative means the opposite)
- **Discretionary Expenses:** Non-essential spending on things you want but don't
  need (e.g. eating out, entertainment, and shopping for clothes)
- **Smart Spending Habits:** Strategies to maximize value from your money and
  make informed spending choices.

## Important Case Studies and Incidents

### Smart Spending

- **Warren Buffett:** Lived in the same house he bought in 1958 for $31.500
  [source typo: the figure is $31,500], despite accumulating mass wealth. Eat
  simple meals instead of dining at fancy restaurants. Invested conservatively
  and avoided unnecessary purchases. His conservative lifestyle led him to become
  one of the richest people in the world with a net worth of $116.9 billion
  dollars.

### Bad Spending

- **Antoine Walker (NBA Star):** Former NBA player who had made over $108 million
  dollars during his career. Instead of making smart purchases and investing his
  money to generate wealth, he overspent on luxury cars, mansions, and
  entourages. He made bad real estate investments that backfired on him. He had
  no financial discipline or long-term goals. As a result, he had to file for
  bankruptcy just two years after retiring.
