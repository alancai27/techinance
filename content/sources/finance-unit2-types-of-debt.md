**Different Types of Debt**

Learning the types of debt and how to manage it can be challenging. However, debt is both a common and important part of personal finance and therefore it is very important that you know the various types, benefits, and risks of debt. Understanding the different types of debt that include but are not limited to **student loans**, **credit card debt**, **mortgages**, and **auto loans** will help individuals such as you make informed decisions and stay away from financial hardship.

**Student loans** are loans that are made to help students pay for higher levels of education such as college. They can either be **federal loans** that are offered by the government or **private loans** that are offered by banks and other lenders. Student loans can always be a great investment for your future but there are risks such as not completing your education or not being able to pay it back in the future. There is also often high over time interest and that can cause repayment problems.

**Credit card debt** is a type of debt known as **revolving debt** which allows individuals to borrow a limited amount of money that must be paid back over time. Although credit cards are very easy to use and can help build good credit, they carry one or if not the highest interest rates among all major debt types. If credit card debt is not fully paid each month, debt will quickly accumulate to the point where it cannot be managed anymore. This makes credit card debt have a high risk of falling into a **debt cycle** in which minimum payments are paid.

**Mortgages** are long-term loans in which individuals use to purchase property. Mortgage loans are usually repaid over a span of 15 to 30 years. However, these loans have relatively low interest rates compared to most other types of debts and if not paid back can result in the property being taken away. This is an example of a **secured loan** as secured loans are backed by collateral that can be taken away such as cars and houses. **Unsecured loans** on the other hand are based on **credit scores** and do not require collateral. The main risks that are associated with mortgages include taking more debt than one can afford to pay back. This can be caused by separate risks such as facing job loss or **economic downturns.**

**Auto loans** are loans that are used by individuals to purchase vehicles such as cars. They are typically loans of shorter lengths than mortgages. However, like mortgages, they are paid back over a period of time with the only difference this time being for a vehicle. One huge risk of auto loans is due to the rapid **depreciation** in value of cars over time. This oftentimes causes situations in which the borrower borrows more than what the car is worth.

In conclusion, there are many different types of debt that can help people satisfy their goals, but it’s important to understand the risks and benefits that are associated with each type of debt. No matter the amount, borrowers should always evaluate their financial situation, consider the total cost of borrowing including interest, and make a decision that ensures that debt remains a tool and not a burden.

  **Key Terms and Facts:** 

* **Student Loans:** Loans that are used by students to pay for education such as college.  
* **Credit Card Debt:** Form of revolving debt that allows individuals to borrow a certain amount of money.  
* **Mortgages:** Long-term loans that are used to purchase property.  
* **Auto Loans:** Shorter-term loans than mortgages that are used to purchase cars.  
* **Federal Loans:** Loans that are offered to individuals by the government.  
* **Private Loans:** Loans that are offered to individuals by banks and other lenders.  
* **Revolving Debt:** An agreement to be able to borrow money repeatedly a set number of times while repaying in installments.  
* **Debt Cycle:** Continuously borrowing money and not paying it back.  
* **Secured Loans:** A loan where a borrower provides collateral that the lender can take if money is not repaid.  
* **Unsecured Loans:** Loans that are not backed up by collateral and instead are reliant on creditworthiness.  
* **Credit Scores:** Numerical score that depicts an individual's creditworthiness and usually ranges from 300 to 850\.  
* **Economic Downturns:** Significant decline in economic activity across an economy.  
* **Depreciation:** Decrease in the value of an item over a period of time.
